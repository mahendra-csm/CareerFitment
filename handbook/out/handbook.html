The test

Is it just
another test?

No. A psychometric career assessment is a structured tool that looks beyond marks to understand how you think, what interests you, and where your natural strengths lie.

The OneGrasp Career Fitment Assessment is built on well-established psychology frameworks. It asks short, everyday questions (what would you do, what do you enjoy, how do you prefer to learn) and reads your answers through those frameworks.

The result is not a single “right career”. It is a clear picture of you, the career fields where that picture fits best, and a plan to explore them in real life.

What it is not

  • Not an exam you can fail. Only the Aptitude section has right answers.
  • Not a label. Results describe preferences today, and people grow.
  • Not a replacement for a conversation. It is the best starting point for one.

What you get back

  • Your profile across 8 dimensions, on one page
  • Your top career areas, each with a fit %
  • Where you are strong and where to grow
  • A long-term roadmap: subjects, exams, degrees, skills, jobs
  • Scholarships, funded routes and resources
  • A page for parents: how to guide, not decide
  • A dashboard of 20+ tools to act on it

One tip before you start

Answer as you really are, not as you think you should be.

There are no good or bad results, only better fits. If you answer the way a parent, teacher or friend would like you to, the report will describe them, not you.

If two options both feel true, pick the one that is true more often.

Don't overthink

Your first honest reaction is usually the most accurate.

Use Next to skip

Answer-quality check

When you submit, the test looks at how the paper was answered: how many answers came in under 3 seconds, and whether the same option was chosen again and again. It never changes your scores. 01

Personality

How do I naturally work and decide?

What it measures

Where you get your energy, how you take in information, how you make decisions and how you like to plan. These patterns shape how you prefer to work with others, handle tasks and make choices.

The framework behind it

Personality-type preferences in four pairs, widely known as MBTI-style typing. Some stages also look at the five everyday traits psychologists call the Big Five (openness, conscientiousness, extraversion, agreeableness, emotional steadiness).

What it looks at

Energy: Introvert or Extravert

Do you recharge alone or with people?

Information: Sensing or Intuition

Do you trust facts and details, or patterns and possibilities?

Decisions: Thinking or Feeling

Do you decide by logic first, or by values and people first?

Planning: Judging or Perceiving

Do you like a plan, or prefer to keep options open?

Good to know: There is no good or bad personality, only better fits. Your type is a preference, and you can act outside it whenever you need to.

Career Interests

What kind of work excites me?

What it measures

Your natural interests and the kinds of work and activities that energise you. Most people are strongest in two or three of six interest types.

The framework behind it

Holland's theory of career interests (RIASEC). People and work environments can both be described with the same six themes, so your code points straight at matching careers.

What it looks at

Realistic (doing)

Hands-on work with tools, machines, nature.

Investigative (thinking)

Research, analysis, solving puzzles.

Artistic (creating)

Design, writing, music, new ideas.

Social (helping)

Teaching, caring, counselling.

Entrepreneurship

Entrepreneurship is best
learned by doing.

Most students meet entrepreneurship as a subject: a chapter, a guest lecture, a case study. OneGrasp's startup programmes give them a structured occasion to take one real problem, build a first answer and defend it in front of people who ask hard questions.

01

SPOT

Observe, investigate and define a real problem.

02

BUILD

Develop a prototype or a structured business solution.

03

PITCH

Present the idea, answer questions and plan the next steps.

From problem to pitch: what students actually do

1Meet a real-world problem
2Find out who lives with it
3Build and test a first solution
4Shape a simple business model
5Present it and answer questions

In the lecture hall

Learning about business: a chapter on business models, a guest lecture, a case study.

In the programme

Building and testing an idea: a real problem chosen by the team, a first solution people can handle, a panel that asks hard questions.

For universities
OneGrasp designs the cohort with the institution and delivers it on campus. A short, well-run programme gives an institution a repeatable way to offer this experience. Talk to us at support@onegrasp.com.

For parents

Guide,
not decide.

Your child's report is a map of who they are today. Your role is to help them read it, test it in real life and make their own choice, with your support.

Do

  • Read the report together, slowly, the first time
  • Start with strengths. Say them out loud.
  • Ask what surprised them, and what felt true
  • Help them try things: a visit, a course, a conversation with someone in the field
  • Check facts together: fees, exams, deadlines
  • Use the dashboard's Decision Room to compare two paths calmly
  • Revisit the report every 6–12 months

Avoid

  • Choosing for them because of marks, relatives or neighbours
  • Treating a lower score as a failure
  • Comparing with siblings or friends
  • Pushing a stream or exam they have no interest in
  • Paying anyone who promises a guaranteed admission, job or scholarship
  • Ending the conversation with “because I said so”

FAQ

Questions people
often ask.

How long does the test take?

You have up to 90 minutes. Answers save as you go, and you can stop and resume.

Can I fail?

No. Only the Aptitude section has right answers, and the report is never pass or fail.

Can I retake it?

Yes. Many students retake it as they move up a stage (for example Class 8, then Class 10, then Class 12).

Who sees my answers?

Only you. Schools see progress and activity, never individual answers.

Is the report accurate?

It reflects your honest answers through established frameworks. A trust check flags rushed tests.

My result doesn't match my dream career. Now what?

A gap is a plan to build, not a no. Use the Career Selector, Test-Drive and My plan.

Can I use it on a phone?

Yes: phone, tablet or laptop. A laptop is easiest for the test.

Are the salaries real?

They are indicative ranges for India, for planning. Actual pay varies.

Are the exams and scholarships up to date?

They link to official sources. Always confirm dates and eligibility there.

Do the tools cost extra?

The dashboard tools are included. Spotlight services are paid; asking is free.

I lost my password.

Use “Forgot password” on the sign-in page for a reset link.
