About us

What is
OneGrasp?

OneGrasp Education Pvt. Ltd. is an education company based in Hyderabad, India. We help students, families, professionals and institutions make better decisions about learning and work.

In simple words
OneGrasp is like a GPS for your career. First it finds where you are today (your strengths, interests and personality). Then it shows the roads that suit you, and finally it gives you turn-by-turn directions: subjects, exams, colleges, skills, money and jobs.

Our best-known product is the Career Fitment Assessment: a psychometric career assessment for students from Class 6 to undergraduates. It measures eight dimensions of a student and turns them into a personal report and a dashboard of more than twenty tools.

Around the assessment, OneGrasp runs international scientific conferences, startup and entrepreneurship programmes, certifications, workshops and internships, one-to-one career counselling, and admissions guidance for India and abroad.

Our belief

Every student is good at something. A career assessment should never label a child. It should show where they will thrive, what to try next, and how to get there with honest facts about exams, colleges, costs and jobs.

Why it matters

Three traps that decide
too many careers.

Ask ten adults why they chose their career and many will say “I didn't really choose.” These are the three most common traps.

Trap 1

Marks

“You scored 95 in maths, so take engineering.”

Marks show how you did in an exam. They do not show what you enjoy, how you work, or what you will want to do for 30 years.

Trap 2

Peer pressure

“All my friends are taking MPC.”

Your friends have different strengths and dreams. Following the crowd feels safe today and costly later.

Trap 3

Guesswork

“My cousin did B.Com, so…”

Most families know only a handful of careers. With 930 careers in our library, guessing means missing most of your options.

What a better decision looks like

Old wayOneGrasp way
Choose by marksChoose by fit: interests + strengths + aptitude
Follow friendsFollow your own profile
Know 5 careersExplore 930 careers
Decide once, aloneDecide in steps, as a family
Hope it worksTest it first: Test-Drive, internships, projects
In simple words
A good career fit is where three circles overlap: what you enjoy (interests), what you are good at (strengths and aptitude) and what the world needs and pays for (real careers). The assessment finds the first two; the dashboard shows you the third.
For parents
Your experience matters, and so does your child's profile. Use the report as a shared, neutral starting point for the conversation.

The test

Is it just
another exam?

No. A psychometric career assessment is a structured set of questions that looks beyond marks to understand how you think, what interests you, and where your natural strengths lie.

In simple words
Psycho means mind and metric means measure. A psychometric assessment measures parts of your mind that an exam does not: your preferences, interests, feelings and ways of thinking. Think of it as a mirror, not a marksheet.

The OneGrasp Career Fitment Assessment asks short, everyday questions: what would you do in this situation, what do you enjoy, how do you like to learn. It reads your answers through well-established psychology frameworks and turns them into a picture of you.

That picture is then matched against hundreds of careers, so the report can say which fields fit you best, and why.

Example
Two students both score 90% in Class 10. One loves taking machines apart and solving puzzles alone; the other loves debating and organising school events. Same marks, very different best-fit careers. Marks could not tell them apart. The assessment can.

The science

Built on frameworks
psychologists trust.

Every dimension is read through a model that has been studied and used for decades. Here is what sits behind each part of your report.

01
Personality

Personality-type preferences (MBTI-style): introvert–extravert, sensing–intuition, thinking–feeling, judging–perceiving. Some stages use the Big Five traits.

Carl Jung's types; Myers & Briggs
02
Career Interests

Holland's RIASEC model: Realistic, Investigative, Artistic, Social, Enterprising, Conventional.

John L. Holland, 1959
03
Multiple Intelligences

Eight ways of being smart: linguistic, logical, spatial, bodily, musical, interpersonal, intrapersonal, naturalistic.

Howard Gardner, 1983
04
Emotional Intelligence

Self-awareness, self-management, social awareness and relationship management.

Salovey & Mayer; Goleman
05
Learning Style

Preferences for seeing, hearing, reading/writing and doing.

VARK-style preference models
06
Motivators

Work values: achievement, learning, mastery, security, leadership, social impact, autonomy, creativity.

Work-values research
07
Strengths

Eight strength domains, using forced-choice situations.

Strengths-based psychology
08
Aptitude

Verbal, numerical, abstract, spatial and logical reasoning, plus data.

Standard reasoning tests
In simple words
A framework is a well-tested way of describing people. It means the same answers always give the same result, and every result can be explained in plain words.
Good to know

Trust

Built to
be trusted.

A report is only useful if it really describes the student. Three safeguards protect every result.

Answer-quality check

When you submit, the test looks at how the paper was answered: how many answers came in under 3 seconds, and whether the same option was picked again and again. It never changes your scores. It only flags a report that may not reflect you (high, medium or low trust), so a rushed test can be retaken.

Your answers stay private

Your report belongs to you. If you joined through a school, the school sees your progress and activity, never your individual answers.

Facts linked to sources

Exams, scholarships, funded routes and funding programmes link to their official websites, so anyone can check them.

For parents

Guide,
not decide.

Your child's report is a map of who they are today. Your role is to help them read it, test it in real life, and make their own choice, with your support.

“A child who chooses a path that fits them works harder, stays longer and recovers faster from setbacks than one who was told what to do.
For parents
Your experience is valuable. Use the report as a neutral third voice at the table, so the conversation is about facts, not who wins.

Do

  • Read the report together, slowly, the first time
  • Start with strengths. Say them out loud.
  • Ask what surprised them, and what felt true
  • Help them try things: a visit, a course, a conversation with someone in the field
  • Check facts together: fees, exams, deadlines
  • Use the Decision Room to compare two paths calmly
  • Revisit the report every 6–12 months

Avoid

  • Choosing for them because of marks, relatives or neighbours
  • Treating a lower score as a failure
  • Comparing with siblings or friends
  • Pushing a stream or exam they have no interest in
  • Paying anyone who promises a guaranteed admission, job or scholarship
  • Ending the talk with “because I said so”
